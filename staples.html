Staples I Keep on Hand

The high-protein basics I always have stocked, so a real meal is never far off.

Tofu
Super firm — for cubing and air frying
Firm — for making scrambled tofu
Silken — for dressings and sauces, and added to smoothies for extra protein
Soy curls
After rehydration, they can be marinated and baked/air fried just like tofu. It makes great vegan jerky pieces!
Vegan protein powders
Orgain — note that not all Orgain products are plant-based, so be sure to check the label
Koia — higher protein, but a serving is a lot — half a cup
Sun Warrior — my personal favorite, and also suitable for raw vegan diets
Vegan protein bars
IQ — soft and chewy
Mezcla — contains crispies
Fello — savory and crunchy
Vegan protein drinks
Pirq — lower calorie but also lower protein
OWYN — check the label; they have an extra-high protein line, so not all products are equal
Orgain — like their protein powders, not all of their protein drinks are plant-based, so read the label carefully before you buy
Quinoa
A relatively high-protein grain, cooks very quickly.
Sprouted oats
Slightly higher in protein than regular rolled oats, but every little bit helps!
Soy milk
My plant milk of choice, as it has more protein than nut and oat milks.
Nutritional yeast
The vegan alternative to cheese, and a good B12 source.
Hummus
I use this mostly as salad dressing.
Dried beans
I keep a variety on hand: black beans, red beans, white beans, chickpeas. They're less expensive than canned beans.
Lentils
Use green or brown lentils for recipes where you want them to retain their shape. Red lentils cook down mushy, and make a great high-protein soup or stew base.
Edamame puffs
Hands-down my favorite protein booster. They're crispy and have no taste—very much like Rice Krispies cereal—and can be used in salads as a replacement for croutons, or an addition to anything that needs some crunch. A serving is 25 grams (about 1/4 cup) and contains 20 grams of protein for only 100 calories.
